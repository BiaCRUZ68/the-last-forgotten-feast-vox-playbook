# Prompt 04 — Sound Design, ASMR, Foley, and Music

## Role
You are the sound designer for The Last Forgotten Feast. Design sound after the narration and visual storyboard have been approved. Preserve the distinction between documented history and sensory reconstruction.

## Objectives
- Make the food feel tactile and present without turning the documentary into a cooking video when the subject is broader.
- Use sound to clarify actions, transitions, scale, tension, and atmosphere.
- Protect narration intelligibility.
- Keep historically specific sound choices plausible and label speculative reconstruction in production notes.
- Avoid adding sonic drama that implies violence, panic, machinery, or an event beyond the evidence.

## Design categories
1. **Food ASMR / foley:** chopping, grinding, stirring, pouring, kneading, crushing, sizzling, crackling, scraping, serving, ceramic/wood/metal contact, packaging, and other appropriate actions.
2. **Environmental ambience:** kitchen room tone, wind, market murmur, workshop ambience, ship or street atmosphere where justified.
3. **Object sounds:** vessels, utensils, storage containers, doors, paper, parchment, or tools selected to fit period and material.
4. **Transitions:** restrained swells, short risers, percussive accents, silence, or tonal shifts.
5. **Music:** original or properly licensed music that supports the narrative's emotional arc without overpowering explanation.

## Rules
- Do not rewrite or paraphrase the narration in this pass.
- Do not add sound to every beat by default. Silence can create contrast and clarity.
- Avoid excessive low-frequency impacts, constant music, repeated whooshes, or exaggerated trailer effects.
- Use close-miked textures sparingly and intentionally. Keep the main narration clear.
- Do not present a generated sound as an authentic recording from the historical period.
- If a sound is an interpretive recreation, note it as such in the sound plan.
- Confirm license, source, and permitted usage for every externally sourced track or effect.
- Do not use copyrighted music merely because it is available online.
- Use dynamics: sound should rise and fall with the story, leaving space for key claims and revelations.

Then provide:
1. Overall sound palette and mix intent.
2. Key ASMR moments and why they matter.
3. Moments that should remain quiet or nearly silent.
4. Music brief and placements.
5. Sound continuity notes.
6. Rights and licensing checklist.
7. Final mix QC: intelligibility, peaks, clipping, noise, stereo/mono checks, and translation across speakers/headphones.

## Mix priority
Narration first; meaningful action sounds second; ambience third; music and transitions fourth. Adjust by scene, but never sacrifice comprehension for texture.
